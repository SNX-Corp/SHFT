Recruitment Kit

Five playbooks in one page. Pull this up at an open house, mid-conversation, or the night before. Not for distribution.

01 · Family Meeting

45 minutes. This meeting is your highest-converting asset and the one thing no other program offers. The structure matters because your instinct will be to talk about the robot for 40 minutes. Don't. Parents decide on trust, fit, and money, in that order.

Before the Meeting
  • They should already know the fee is $8,000. It's on the site, the poster, and the deck. The meeting is never where the price is revealed; it is where the price is explained. If they booked before seeing it, send the fee sheet with the booking confirmation.
  • Have ready: the fee sheet, the season calendar, the application QR, and your phone with photos of the shop.
The 5-Step Shape
Step 1 · Listen First (10 min)

Open with questions, not a pitch. You are diagnosing fit, and parents trust people who ask about their kid before talking about themselves.

"Before I say anything about SHFT, tell me about [student's name]. What do they do when nobody's assigning them anything?"

Follow-ups: What are they into at school? Have they built or coded anything? What does their week look like (other sports, tutoring, music)? What are you hoping this does for them?

Write the answers down in front of them. You will use their words in step 2.

Step 2 · The Program, Through Their Kid (10 min)

Not the generic pitch. Connect what they just told you to specifics:

"You said he loves Minecraft redstone but has never touched a real tool. That's most of our students on day one. Here's his first month: safety training, then the CAD course, then he's machining a real part by NEEDS INPUT: realistic milestone."

Cover, briefly: the weekly schedule (3-4 days, 15-20 hours, the actual days NEEDS INPUT: the actual days), the season arc (training from September, build season January, competitions spring), who is in the room (you, Noeen, the ratio), and safety (tool training before tool use, supervision, VERIFY: screening and insurance language once confirmed).

Step 3 · The Credibility 60 Seconds (not longer)

"The honest version of who we are: SHFT has never played a match. What you are betting on is coaching. Noeen and I run Team 7558 ALT-F4 here in Toronto. When we started there, the team was 35th in Canada. This year it finished 3rd, with a 213 and 96 record over seven seasons. That record is public. Look up team 7558 on The Blue Alliance tonight and check me on it. Our goal for SHFT's first season is a top-10 finish in Ontario. That's a goal, not a promise. The promise is the program: the hours, the tools, the coaching."

Then stop talking about yourself.

Step 4 · The Money Moment (5 min)

Say the number yourself, plainly, before they have to ask. Do not soften it with "investment." Do not apologize for it.

"The fee is $8,000 for the year. Here's exactly what that covers: registration for two district events, all parts and materials, the tools, the workshop, food at meetings, and the coaching. The only thing on top is hotels and travel if the team advances to the provincial championship.

You can pay in full, by term, or monthly at $800 over ten months NEEDS INPUT: confirm plan. The deposit is $500 and it comes off the fee. If we somehow can't field the team, every dollar comes back. If [student's name] starts in September and it's not right, here's the refund window in writing. [Hand them the sheet.]

And I want to say this part out loud: if the number is the obstacle and the kid is right for this, talk to me. We hold a couple of reduced-fee seats VERIFY: only say once funded. That conversation is private and it's safe to have."

Then be quiet. Let them react. Silence after a price is not a problem to fix.

Step 5 · Close Without Pressure (5 min)

"Here's what happens next if you want in: the application takes ten minutes, the deposit holds the seat, and we cap the team at 25, so when it's full it's full. Enrollment stays open until December, so you don't have to decide tonight. What questions do I still owe you answers to?"

Same evening: send the thank-you email (see Section 04, template 5) with the fee sheet, the application link, and one personal line about their kid.

02 · Objection Playbook

The rule for every answer: agree with the truth inside the objection first, then give the receipt. Never argue a parent out of a real concern.

+"Eight thousand dollars is a lot of money."
"It is. It's in the range of rep hockey or a year of competitive dance, and I want you to compare us to those honestly. The difference is where the money goes: parts, machines, registration, and coaching. Here's the breakdown line by line. And there's a monthly plan, which is how most families do it."

Never say "families who understand the value." Never.

+"You're a brand-new team. Why would I trust you with this?"
"You shouldn't trust us on our word. Check the record instead: Noeen and I coach Team 7558, and the climb from 35th to 3rd in Canada is publicly logged on The Blue Alliance. SHFT is us running the same playbook from day one. And the refund terms are in writing precisely because you are right to want protection from a year-one program."
+"Is it safe? There are real machines in there."
"Yes, and that worry is the correct one to have. No student touches a tool they haven't been trained and signed off on. There's always NEEDS INPUT: ratio adults in the shop. VERIFY: add screening, insurance, first aid. Come see the shop before you decide; bring [student's name]."
+"My kid has no experience."
"Nobody on a rookie team has experience; that's what rookie means. The whole fall is training: safety, CAD, tools, code. The program is built for beginners. What I can't coach is wanting to be there, and from what you've told me, that part's already handled."
+"What about schoolwork? 15 to 20 hours is huge."
"It's the realest objection there is, and I'd rather lose a signup than pretend otherwise. The load peaks January and February. Here's the weekly calendar; put it next to their other commitments. If this year is the wrong year, tell me, and I'll hold a conversation for next season instead of selling you this one."
+"Why not the school team / why does the school version cost nothing?"
"School teams are great, and if their school has one, look at it seriously. Most in our area don't, or cap their rosters. A community team means nobody's school decides whether they get to build. The fee exists because there's no school board paying for the shop, parts, or registration; you're seeing the real cost that's invisible at a school."
+"What does my kid actually walk away with?"
"Concrete things: a CAD portfolio, code they wrote, a robot they can point to and say 'I made that part,' and competition experience explaining their work to judges. I won't promise university admissions; nobody honestly can. The work speaks for itself in interviews, and we write reference letters that describe exactly what the student did."
+"What if they quit halfway?"
"Here's the refund schedule in writing. [Walk through it.] And before build season I'll tell you honestly if it isn't working, because a miserable student in the shop in February helps nobody, including the team."
The meta-rule: Any question you can't answer with a receipt gets "I don't know yet; we're a first-year team. Here's what I do know." Honesty about year one is your differentiation, not your weakness.
03 · Open House
Sunday, June 21, 2026 · 3:00 to 5:00 PM · 26 Mallard Rd, North York

Audience: parents and kids, both FIRST-aware and never-heard-of-it. Your job is not to perform. It is to let the shop, the robot, and the receipts do the talking while you have twenty short conversations.

Layout (Set Up the Day Before)
  • Sign-in table at the door. Name, email, phone, student grade, "how did you hear about us." This list IS the event's output; everything else is atmosphere. QR to the same form for the line.
  • Station 1: the robot. ALT-F4's robot (with 7558's permission), with a printed card: "This is Vision, our sister team's 2025 robot. SHFT students build their own starting January." Honest labeling, every time.
  • Station 2: the tools. Two benches live: one with CAD on a screen, one with a simple hands-on task kids can actually do (drive practice if allowed, or drill/rivet a bracket with PPE). Kids who DO something make parents who sign things.
  • Station 3: the money and calendar table. Fee sheet stacks, season calendar, refund terms. Unstaffed and unavoidable. Parents read price sheets when nobody is watching them read.
  • Snacks. Music low. Doors open 15 minutes early because parents arrive early.

10 Questions Parents Will Ask (30-Second Answers)

Practice these out loud, not in your head.

"How much does it cost?"

$8,000 for the year. Registration, parts, tools, facility, food, coaching, all in. Payment plans available, terms in writing at that table.

"Is it safe?"

No student touches a tool without training and sign-off. There are always NEEDS INPUT: N trained adults in the shop. VERIFY: add screening/insurance. Come look at the shop rules on the wall.

"My kid has zero experience. Really okay?"

That's the design. Fall is all training: safety, CAD, code. Rookie team means everyone starts at zero together.

"How many hours a week?"

Three to four days, 15 to 20 hours, heaviest in January and February. Here's the calendar; check it against hockey and homework before you commit. I mean that.

"Who are you?"

I'm the head coach. Noeen and I also coach Team 7558 ALT-F4, which went from 35th in Canada to 3rd. Look up 7558 on The Blue Alliance tonight; the record's public.

"Will SHFT be any good?"

Honestly: no one knows, we're a rookie team. Our goal is top 10 in Ontario, and it's a goal, not a promise. What I can promise is the program: the hours, the tools, the coaching.

"Does this help with university?"

I won't promise admissions. What students leave with is concrete: a CAD portfolio, code, a robot they built, and mentors who write reference letters about what they actually did.

"What if it's not for them?"

Refund terms are in writing: NEEDS INPUT: refund summary. And I'll tell you honestly before build season if it isn't working.

"Whose robot is that?"

Our sister team's, Team 7558. SHFT students build their own starting in January. We borrowed it so you could see what the kids are building toward.

"Why is it $8,000 when school teams are free?"

School teams have a board paying the invisible costs. We don't, so you see the real number. It's the rep-hockey range, and here's the line-by-line.

Practice Protocol (12 Days)
The Setup

You don't need to become a speaker. You need 12 minutes of material you've said out loud enough times that your brain is free to look at people.

3 recorded run-throughs minimum. Phone propped up, full talk, no stopping. Watch each one once. You are checking exactly three things: Did I run past 12 minutes? Did I say um/like more than once a sentence? Did I rush the money slide?

The 5 Delivery Habits
  1. When you'd say "um," close your mouth instead. A pause reads as confidence; filler reads as nerves. Same length of silence.
  2. Stand still on the numbers. Feet planted for "35th to 3rd" and "$8,000." Move between points, not during them.
  3. Say the fee slide SLOWER than every other slide, then stop talking for two full seconds. Do not flinch after the number.
  4. Pick three parents in different corners; rotate eye contact between them. The room reads it as everyone.
  5. Hands hold the clicker or hang. No pockets, no arm-crossing.
Memorization Rule + Dress Rehearsal

Don't memorize sentences; memorize the slide order. The speaker notes in the deck are spoken English on purpose. If you blank, read the slide headline out loud and keep going; nobody knows the script but you.

Dress rehearsal in the real room the day before, with Noeen as the audience playing the hostile question (price, safety, rookie risk). One run, then stop. Over-rehearsing the night before adds nerves, not polish.

Plan B (If June 21 Slips)

The December deadline means June is the warm-up, not the final. If the date is not certain by June 12, move it deliberately instead of scrambling:

  • Announce "Open houses: July [X] and September [Y]" so a slip reads as a schedule, not a cancellation.
  • The September event is the big one regardless (back-to-school is when activity decisions happen). Budget your best promotion push for it.
  • In the meantime, offer standing shop visits: "Text 647-878-6114 to walk through the shop any Saturday." One family at a time converts better than zero events.

05 · House Style Card

The 60-second check before anything ships. Run every draft through all ten.

  • 01Does every claim have a number, a name, or an address in it? If not, find the receipt or cut the claim.
  • 02Is the $8,000 fee visible before any ask? If money appears (referral, discount, "investment"), the fee must already be on the page.
  • 03Search the draft for: world-class, professional-grade, premier, launchpad, elevate, high-impact, future-focused, cutting-edge, "not just," "isn't another." Any hit: rewrite.
  • 04Read every sentence under four words aloud. If it sounds like a movie trailer, join it to a real sentence.
  • 05Does anything promise a result (win, trophies, stay for years, fluent, universities recognize)? Only the top-10 GOAL survives, framed as a goal.
  • 06Does anything stand on another team or club to look taller? Cut it.
  • 07Tense check: is anything not-yet-real written in present tense? Rewrite as "we are working to..." or date it.
  • 08Zero em dashes. One tagline per artifact: "Shift the standard."
  • 09Would the most stretched parent in the room feel smaller reading this? Fix that line.
  • 10Could a Chief Delphi skeptic screenshot any line and dunk on it? That line goes.
