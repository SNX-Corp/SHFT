# SHFT Public Gate: The Mentor Voice

SHFT has taken real backlash for content that read as AI slop, overconfident,
borderline untrue, or overpromising. This gate exists so that never happens again.
It applies to EVERY word and pixel an outside person sees under the SHFT name,
no exceptions, whether Malhar asked for it or not.

## Who is speaking

The voice is a mentor, not a marketer. Picture a coach who has actually built
robots with kids for years, standing in front of parents at a kitchen-table info
night. That person:

- Explains, never sells. Invites, never pressures.
- Uses short plain sentences. Says "we" and "I" meaning real people with names.
- Is comfortable saying "we're new," "we don't know yet," and "that's not
  included." Honesty about being early IS the credibility.
- Talks about the kids' work, not the program's greatness.
- Names prices plainly and makes it safe to say no.
- Would be fine if a veteran FIRST mentor or a rival program read every line.

## The four gates (each one caused real damage - all must pass)

### Gate 1: No AI slop
Kill every machine tell before it ships. The big ones:
- Negation-elevation: "not just a class, a community." State the thing plainly.
- Staccato fragments: "Real robots. Real skills. Real fun." Write sentences.
- Empty status words: world-class, cutting-edge, unlock, elevate, empower,
  journey, passion, thrive, next-level, game-changing. Replace with a fact or cut.
- Hollow triads and manufactured inspiration ("build the future").
- Em dashes. Never.
For a full sweep, run /deslop (covers visual slop in design too).

### Gate 2: No overconfidence
- No superlatives, ever: not "best," "premier," "leading," "top." SHFT earns
  trust by receipts, not rank claims.
- Goals stated as goals: "our goal this season is X," never "we will win X."
- Calibrated language is the default: "so far," "in our first year," "we've
  found," "we think."
- Never punch down at other programs, schools, or clubs to look taller.

### Gate 3: No borderline lying
- Every factual claim needs a receipt Malhar can produce on demand: a number,
  a name, a date, a photo, a link. No receipt = cut it or mark it `VERIFY:` and
  stop for his answer. Never fill the gap with a bigger adjective.
- Never imply scale, history, or a track record SHFT does not have. No "trusted
  by families across the city," no invented student counts, no implied years of
  operation, no testimonials that don't exist word-for-word.
- Numbers are exact or absent. Not "hundreds," not "countless."

### Gate 4: No overpromising
- Promise only what is already secured and deliverable today. Everything else
  is written in honest tense: "we are working to," "we plan to," "if we get X."
- Never guarantee outcomes for a child: no promised skills, awards, portfolio
  results, or university advantages. Describe what students DO in the program;
  let parents draw conclusions.
- No guilt levers or urgency theater: no "your child deserves," no "investment
  in their future," no fake scarcity or countdowns unless the limit is real
  (then state the real number and why).

## Think like an outside observer, not an engineer

(Noeen's HR advisor, July 2026: "stop thinking like engineers and robotics
experts; think like businessmen and outside observers.") Applied to copy:
- Frame everything in terms an outsider cares about: what their kid does, what
  it costs, who's coaching, what a season looks like. Not mechanisms, specs,
  or robotics jargon (no unexplained FRC/CAD/auton talk on parent-facing pages;
  one plain-words gloss on first use).
- Before shipping, ask: what does this look like to someone who knows nothing
  about us and owes us nothing? That reader decides everything.

## The mentor test (final pass, always)

Read the finished piece as three people:
1. The most skeptical parent in the room, who has seen AI slop before and is
   stretched on money. Does any line make them roll their eyes or feel small?
2. A veteran FIRST mentor. Does any claim make them wince?
3. Malhar in six months, when a parent quotes this line back to him and asks
   him to make good on it. Is he comfortable?

If any reader flinches, rewrite before showing anything.

## Confidentiality matrix (hard rules, override everything)

BVG (the school that hosts ALT-F4 and employs Noeen) is strict and lawyer-like
about conflicts of interest. The community knows these links exist; our rule is
that they never exist IN WRITING from us. A written link risks Noeen's job.

- **ALT-F4 name/IP is protected.** Never write ALT-F4's name, team number 7558,
  logo, or competition record on SHFT, SNX, or WCP materials. Never write SHFT
  on ALT-F4 materials. No "our mentors built ALT-F4" style credibility borrowing.
- **Noeen Kashif appears publicly ONLY as ALT-F4's coach.** His name never
  appears on SHFT, SNX, or WCP content: not in bios, not in leadership sections,
  not in metadata, not in image filenames. His SHFT partnership and WCP
  relationship are under the table; his name is deliberately absent from the
  SHFT NPO and hidden in SNX.
- **"WCP team" is the shareable accolade.** SHFT may publicly say it is
  WCP-sponsored, and ALT-F4 may too, but never pair them ("both teams") in the
  same sentence, page, or post. Each team claims it independently.
- SHFT credibility is built from: WCP sponsorship, Malhar's own named
  experience, unattributed mentor experience ("our mentors have coached FRC and
  VEX for 10+ years"), and what students actually do. Never from ALT-F4's
  trajectory by name.
