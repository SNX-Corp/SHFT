### 1. How much does it cost?

### 2. How much time does it take?
During the build season (about six weeks in the new year), it's the most intensive: expect [FILL: e.g., a few evenings a week plus some weekends]. Outside of build season it's lighter, around [FILL: e.g., one to two meetings a week]. Students can ramp up to the level that fits their schedule. We'd rather have a committed student a few hours a week than burn anyone out.

### 3. Is it safe?
Yes. Safety is non-negotiable. Students are supervised by mentors, they're trained before using any tool or machine, and we follow FIRST's safety standards along with our own shop rules. Protective equipment is required where it's needed, and nobody touches the robot or a machine without being cleared first.

### 4. My kid has zero experience. Is that okay?
Completely okay, and it's the norm. Most of our members start with no experience at all. We teach the skills: CAD, programming, electrical, strategy, and more. The only thing a student needs to bring is the willingness to learn and show up.

For competitions, we coordinate group travel and share details well in advance."] We'll always give you dates and logistics early so you can plan. If transportation is a real barrier, talk to us and we'll see what we can arrange.

### 6. Does this actually help with college?
Yes, meaningfully. FRC is well respected by engineering and STEM programs, and it opens the door to FIRST scholarships (millions of dollars are available across the program each year). More than that, students walk away with real projects, real skills, and a story they can tell in an application or interview. This is the line that changes the conversation.

### 7. What will my child actually learn?
Real, transferable skills: engineering and CAD, programming, electrical and wiring, plus project management, teamwork, and communication. Students who lean toward the non-technical side learn strategy, scouting, media, and outreach. They learn to build something real under a deadline, which is a skill that carries into any field.

### 8. Who runs the team? Are the mentors qualified?
SHFT was built by mentors with a proven track record in Canadian FRC, including taking a team from nothing to the top of the province. Students are mentored, not left on their own. You're welcome to meet the mentors at the info night and ask them anything.

### 9. What's the time commitment for me as a parent?
Less than you might think, and it's flexible. Parents are welcome but not required at meetings. Where families love to help is volunteering at events, providing meals during build season, or lending a skill. We'll tell you about opportunities, and you choose what fits.

### 10. What if my child tries it and it's not for them?
That's fine, and it happens. We'd rather a student find out by trying than wonder. There's no penalty for stepping back. Many students who come in unsure end up the most committed once they find their corner of the team, whether that's the robot, the code, or the strategy board.
