## 1. Strategy + The Big Idea

We are a brand-new Ontario FRC team that recruits like a top-tier program before we have a single trophy, by selling a credible promise: serious mentorship (proven by ALT-F4 and WCP), real engineering you can put on a resume, and a crew that takes you seriously from day one. The entire 8-week push funnels strangers into one room (the info night) and one room into a roster, using social proof and momentum instead of money.

**The single big idea:** **"Shift the standard."** We are not another after-school robotics club. We are a launchpad that lets you compete from day one. Newcomers hear "you belong and you'll matter fast." Builders hear "finally, a team that's as serious as you are." Both join because SHFT treats joining as joining something that's going somewhere, not signing up for a class.

## 2. Audience

Three people we are actually talking to. Write every post, poster, and DM for one of them.

### Persona A: The Curious Newcomer ("Maya")
- **Who:** Grade 9–10. Likes the idea of robots, has never built one. Scared it's only for "the kids who already know how to code."
- **What she cares about:** Will I be welcome? Will I look dumb? Will I have friends here? Is there a place for me if I'm into design / video / business / not just wiring?
- **Her fear:** "Everyone there will be better than me."
- **Message that moves her:** *"You don't need experience. You need to show up. We'll teach you the rest, and you'll touch a real robot in week one."* Lead with belonging and low barrier. Show beginners doing real things.

### Persona B: The Experienced Builder ("Dev")
- **Who:** Grade 10–12. Already codes, CADs, or wrenches. Maybe bored on an existing team, or his school has nothing serious.
- **What he cares about:** Will I be challenged? Are the mentors actually good? Will I get real responsibility and real tools, or babysitting? Will this look good for university / scholarships?
- **His fear:** "This will be a beginner club where I carry everyone."
- **Message that moves him:** *"Mentors who took a team from nothing to the top of Canadian FRC. Pro-grade tools. Real ownership from day one. Top 10 in Ontario, year one."* Lead with ambition, mentorship credibility (ALT-F4, WCP), and the resume payoff.

### Persona C: The Parent ("Mr./Ms. Singh")
- **Who:** Parent of a 14–17 year old, deciding whether to give time, rides, and possibly money.
- **What they care about:** Is this safe and well-run? Is it worth the hours? Does it help my kid get into university / build skills? Who are the adults in the room? What's the cost and commitment?
- **Their fear:** "Is this a real, organized program or a few teenagers winging it?"
- **Message that moves them:** *"A professional-grade STEM program led by proven, vetted mentors. Real engineering, teamwork, and leadership experience that strengthens university applications. Clear schedule, clear expectations, supported by ALT-F4 and WCP."* Lead with structure, safety, credibility, outcomes.

| **1. Awareness** | They learn SHFT exists and feel the vibe | IG/TikTok, posters, school announcements, word of mouth | **"Follow @shftrobotics"** |
| **2. Interest** | They raise a hand / give us a contact | Link-in-bio form, DMs, info night RSVP | **"Fill the interest form"** (link in bio + poster QR) |
| **3. Join** | They show up and commit | **Info Night** → onboarding / tryout | **"Come to Info Night [FILL: date/time/location]"** |
| **4. Retain** | They stay past week 3 (the drop-off cliff) | First builds, roles, group chat | **"Claim your role / first build task"** |

**Funnel rules:**
- Every poster, bio, and video ends with exactly one CTA for that stage. Never a menu.
- The **interest form** is the spine of the whole plan. Build it Week 1. Fields: name, grade, school, email, parent email, "what are you into" (build / code / design / media / business), how they heard about us. Keep it under 60 seconds.
- Link-in-bio (Linktree or a `/join` page on shftrobotics.com) holds: interest form, Info Night RSVP, Instagram, site.

## 6. Channel Playbook

### Instagram & TikTok (@shftrobotics)
- **Cadence:** 3 posts/week minimum + Stories almost daily during Weeks 5–7. Consistency beats polish.
- **Format mix:** Reels/TikToks (reach) > Carousels (depth) > single images (least reach). Lead with motion and faces.
- **First 1 second wins.** Hook on screen text immediately: "A new Ontario FRC team," "POV: you actually build the robot," "No experience? Good."
- **Content buckets:** (1) Mentor/credibility, (2) Tools & real engineering, (3) Newcomer-welcome, (4) Crew/culture, (5) Parent-trust, (6) Info Night hype.
- **Every post:** one CTA in caption + a clear link path (link in bio → form). Use location tag (your city) and a small fixed hashtag set (#FRC #Ontario #robotics + your own #ShftRobotics).
- **Cross-post:** make it once, post to both IG Reels and TikTok. Push the best clips to Stories.
- **Leverage partners:** ask ALT-F4 and WCP to repost. Their audiences are exactly your audience and it's free credibility.
- **Reply to everything.** Every comment and DM is a recruit. Move them to the form fast and friendly.

### School Postering
- **Get permission first.** Most schools require admin/main-office sign-off and a stamp before anything goes on a wall. Ask in Week 2 and don't skip this: unapproved posters get torn down and burn goodwill.
- **Where they actually get seen:** main entrance, cafeteria, library, science/tech/shop hallways, near the gym, and the official club/announcement boards.
- **Poster design:** logo + "Shift the standard." + one line ("New Ontario FRC team. No experience needed.") + **big QR code** to the form + Info Night date once set. Readable from 2 meters. One CTA.
- **Maintain them:** walk the halls weekly, replace torn/covered posters. A dead poster is worse than none.
- **Combine with PA announcements.** A poster they've already seen plus hearing it on the announcements is the combo that converts.

### Feeder Middle-School Outreach
- **Why:** Grade 8s choosing a high school / first club are your easiest long-term pipeline, and parents are highly engaged at that age.
- **How:** email the middle-school STEM/robotics/science lead, offer a short demo or a few posters. Always go through the teacher/admin. Never DM minors directly.
- **The pitch to them:** "We'll show your students what real robotics looks like, free." Bring a robot or parts; let them touch something.
- **Capture:** parent-facing form (parent email field) since middle-school sign-ups run through parents.

### Word of Mouth / Referral
- **The #1 recruiting tool is each member's friend group.** Make it a structured ask, not a hope.
- **The 2-invite rule:** every existing member personally invites 2 specific people to Info Night (Week 3+). Track names.
- **Make sharing effortless:** give members a ready-to-paste DM and a Story template to repost. "Come check out the team I'm on: info night [date], no experience needed."
- **Founder advantage:** "founding member" is a real selling point. Let people pitch being part of something from day one.

## 7. Budget: 3 Tiers

### Tier 0: Scrappy ($0)
- Free Canva poster, printed on a school/library printer.
- Phone-shot Reels/TikToks, free editing (CapCut).
- Free link-in-bio (Linktree) + free Google Form.
- School PA announcements + word of mouth + partner reposts.
- **Buys you:** a complete, runnable funnel. This alone can fill a roster.

### Tier 1: ~$150
- Everything in Tier 0, plus:
- ~$40–60 pro color poster printing (large format + 30–40 copies).
- ~$30–40 stickers (logo) as a free Info Night handout and walking ad.
- ~$30 small boosted IG/TikTok post targeted to your city + age 14–18.
- ~$20 light snacks for Info Night (raises turnout and stay-time).
- **Buys you:** noticeably better reach + a more legit, higher-converting Info Night.

### Tier 2: ~$500
- Everything in Tier 1, plus:
- ~$150 a few branded t-shirts/hoodies for the core team (members become billboards; founding-crew swag drives word of mouth).
- ~$100–150 stronger paid social over the full 8 weeks.
- ~$80 banner / pull-up sign for Info Night and school events.
- ~$50 better Info Night spread.
- ~$50 a couple of props/demo parts to make the table interactive.
- **Buys you:** a program that looks established and reaches well beyond organic. The "are these guys actually serious?" answer is visibly yes.

> Spend order if money is tight: form (free) → posters/QR → Info Night snacks → boosted post → swag. Don't spend a dollar before the free funnel exists.

## 9. Risks + "If We Are Behind" Recovery Plan

| Risk | Early warning sign | Move |
|---|---|---|
| **No school postering permission** | Admin says no / delays | Go all-in on word of mouth, PA announcements, partner reposts, and feeder-school teacher emails. Posters are nice, not required. |
| **Low social reach** | Followers flat after Week 3 | Stop posting polished stuff; post raw, fast, face-forward Reels. Beg ALT-F4/WCP for reposts. Add one ~$30 boost. |
| **Low Info Night RSVPs** | <15 RSVPs by Week 5 | Switch to direct asks: every member personally invites 3, not 2. DM every single liker/commenter. Move Info Night a few days later if needed. |
| **Low Info Night turnout** | RSVPs don't show | Same-day reminder DMs + texts the morning of. Have members literally walk friends to the room. |
| **People join then ghost** | Drop-off after week 2–3 | Give every new member a real role and a first build task in week one. Active group chat. "Crew not a class" has to be true, not just a slogan. |
| **Key info still unknown** | `[FILL]` blanks remain | Lock **team number, city, Info Night date/time/location, and tryout dates** before Week 3. The calendar and posters break without them. |

**The "we are behind" 1-week sprint** (run if you're under target with 2 weeks left):
1. **Personal asks beat ads.** Every member DMs/texts 5 specific people with a personal invite. This single move fills more seats than anything else.
2. **Move the date if you must.** A well-attended Info Night a week later beats an empty one on time.
3. **Lower the barrier to zero.** "Just come once, no commitment, no experience." Get them in the room; convert in person.
4. **Lean on credibility hard.** Front-load ALT-F4 / WCP / "mentors who took a team to the top of Canadian FRC" in every message. It's your strongest asset when momentum is low.
5. **Capture everyone.** Anyone who shows interest goes on the form and into the group chat the same day. A contact you don't capture is a recruit you lose.
