# Voice

The editorial standard for all of this lives in the "Writing voice and judgment"
section of `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`. That is the single source of truth: the four
lenses, the specificity principle, the no-em-dash rule, and the final pass. This
skill does not restate it. It assumes you are already holding it.

What this skill adds: the brand profiles and the ready patterns the standard does
not carry.

## Quick recap of the standard (the full version is in CLAUDE.md)

- Specificity is the cure. Trade every adjective for the receipt. If you lack the
  receipt, ask Malhar for it. Do not paper the gap with a bigger word.
- Kill the machine tells: negation-elevation, staccato fragments, status words,
  hollow triads, manufactured inspiration, unearned superlatives.
- Be kind to the reader with the least. No guilt levers. Name money plainly.
- Confidence without contempt. Never stand on a competitor to look taller.
- Only claim what is true now. Aspirations in honest tense. Flag shaky facts as
  "VERIFY: ...".
- No em dashes, ever. Restructure instead.

## Ready patterns

These are scaffolds, not fill-in-the-blank forms. Every bracket is a place where
a real detail goes. If you cannot fill a bracket with something true and specific,
ask Malhar rather than reaching for an adjective. Each pattern already obeys the
standard: no em dashes, no unearned superlatives, claims backed by the receipt.

### Sponsor outreach (cold)

> Subject: Partnering with [TEAM], FRC Team [NUMBER]
>
> Hi [NAME],
>
> I'm [YOUR NAME] from [TEAM], a FIRST Robotics team at [SCHOOL] in [CITY]. In
> seven seasons our students have taken the team from 35th to 3rd in Canada.
>
> [ONE specific sentence on why this sponsor: a real product or value link, not
> flattery.]
>
> A partnership puts your name on the robot and our competition kit, in front of
> [VERIFY: real audience figure] people at events. It also funds the parts and
> travel that let the next group of students build something real.
>
> Could I send our sponsor package, or find 15 minutes this week?
>
> Thank you,
> [NAME]

### Sponsor thank-you

> Hi [NAME],
>
> Thank you for backing [TEAM] this season. Your support paid for [SPECIFIC:
> the part, the trip, the tool], which we could not have reached on our own.
>
> [ONE concrete thing it made possible. A result, a build, a moment.]
>
> We will keep you posted as the season goes. You helped build this.
>
> [NAME]

### Social caption

> [Hook: one real result or moment, stated plainly.]
> [One line of context with a real number.]
> [Credit or tag.]

Three or four lines. Lead with the strongest true fact. The ALT-F4 tagline
"Innovate. Build. Compete." may close it, but only when it earns the space.

### Award or impact writeup

Claim, then proof, then the human result. Nothing else.

> [Claim in one sentence.] [The number or named outcome that backs it.] [The
> concrete effect on a student or the community.]

No adjective should do the job a number can do.
